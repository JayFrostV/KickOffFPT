II. Software Requirement Specification
1. Overall Requirements
1.1 Context Diagram
The Quiz Application is an internal training assessment system designed to support knowledge
assessment activities within a corporate training environment.
The system enables Trainers to create and manage quizzes, including their questions and answers.
Trainees can access available quizzes, answer the questions, and submit their responses for
assessment.
The initial release focuses on quiz management and quiz participation. Advanced training
management capabilities, such as assigning quizzes to individual Trainees, maintaining assessment
history, reporting, and analytics, are outside the scope of this release.
Figure 1. Quiz Application Context Diagram
The context diagram illustrates the system boundary and the interactions between the Quiz
Application and its external actors. Trainers provide and maintain quiz-related information, while
Trainees access available quizzes and submit their answers through the system.

1.2 Main Business Processes
The Quiz Application supports two main business processes: Quiz Management and Quiz Participation.
The Quiz Management process enables Trainers to maintain the quiz content used for internal training
assessments. The Quiz Participation process enables Trainees to access available quizzes, answer
questions, and submit their responses.
These processes represent the primary interactions between the system and its users within the scope
of the initial release.
1.2.1 Quiz Management Process
The Quiz Management process enables a Trainer to create and maintain quizzes used for training
assessments. A quiz consists of general quiz information, questions, and their corresponding answers.
The process begins when the Trainer accesses quiz management and selects an appropriate
management action. Depending on the selected action, the Trainer can create a new quiz, modify an
existing quiz, or delete an existing quiz.
When creating or updating a quiz, the Trainer provides or modifies the quiz information and manages
its associated questions and answers. The system validates the provided data before applying the
requested changes. The relationship between a quiz, its questions, and their answers must remain
consistent throughout the operation.
If the provided data is valid, the system applies the changes and informs the Trainer that the operation
has been completed successfully. If the data is invalid or the requested quiz cannot be found, the
system rejects the operation and provides an appropriate error response.
Figure 2. Quiz Management Business Process
1.2.2 Quiz Participation Process
The Quiz Participation process enables a Trainee to participate in an available quiz as part of an internal
training assessment.
The process begins when the Trainee accesses the available quizzes and selects a quiz to participate
in. The system retrieves and presents the selected quiz together with its questions and the information
required for the Trainee to complete the quiz.

The Trainee provides answers to the quiz questions and submits the completed quiz. The system
validates the submitted information to ensure that the submission can be processed.
If the submission is valid, the system accepts the Trainee's responses and confirms that the quiz has
been submitted successfully. If the submission is invalid, the system rejects the submission and
provides an appropriate error response so that the Trainee can correct the relevant information when
applicable.
The initial release does not include quiz assignment to individual Trainees, assessment history,
reporting, or analytics.

1.4 Database Analysis and Design
The database of the Quiz Application is designed to support the two main business areas defined
within the current system scope: Quiz Management and Quiz Participation.
The database maintains the structure and content of quizzes, including quiz information, questions,
and predefined answers. It also provides the basic data structure required to represent quiz
submissions and the responses submitted for individual questions during quiz participation.
The database design focuses only on the data required by the current system scope. Advanced training
management features such as user authentication, quiz assignment, scoring, assessment history,
reporting, and analytics are not included in the current database design.
1.4.1 Database Analysis
Based on the business processes and use cases defined in the previous sections, the data requirements
of the Quiz Application are divided into two main areas: Quiz Management Data and Quiz Participation
Data.
Quiz Management Data
The Quiz Management process requires the system to maintain the structure and content of each quiz.
A Quiz stores the general information required to describe an assessment. Each Quiz may contain
multiple Questions, while each Question belongs to one Quiz.
A Question stores the content of an assessment item and its question type. The question type
determines how the question is presented and answered. The supported question types are:
• Multiple Choice
• Single Choice
• True/False
• Fill in the Blanks
• Short Answer
• Long Answer
Depending on its question type, a Question may contain multiple predefined Answers. An Answer
stores the answer content and indicates whether the answer is considered correct where applicable.
The relationships between Quiz, Question, and Answer must remain consistent when quiz content is
created, updated, or deleted.
* Quiz Participation Data
The Quiz Participation process requires the system to represent a completed quiz submission and the
responses provided for individual questions.
A QuizSubmission represents a submitted attempt for a specific Quiz. Each Quiz may be associated
with multiple QuizSubmissions, while each QuizSubmission belongs to one Quiz.
A SubmissionAnswer represents a response provided for an individual Question within a
QuizSubmission. Each SubmissionAnswer belongs to one QuizSubmission and refers to one Question.
ANG.M.A001.Opt1 - SRS Document Page 7
=== Page 10/21 ===
Depending on the question type, a SubmissionAnswer may reference a predefined Answer or store a
textual response. This structure allows the system to represent responses for both predefined-answer
questions and text-based questions without introducing scoring or assessment-history functionality.
Based on these data requirements, the following five main entities are identified:
# Entity Purpose
1 Quiz Represents the general information of a quiz used for internal training
assessment.
2 Question Represents a question belonging to a quiz and identifies its question type.
3 Answer Represents a predefined answer associated with a question.
4 QuizSubmission Represents a submitted attempt for a quiz during quiz participation.
5 SubmissionAnswer Represents a response provided for an individual question within a quiz
submission.
The main relationships among the identified entities are as follows:
• One Quiz may contain zero or more Questions, while each Question belongs to one Quiz.
• One Question may contain zero or more predefined Answers, while each Answer belongs to
one Question.
• One Quiz may be associated with zero or more QuizSubmissions, while each QuizSubmission
belongs to one Quiz.
• One QuizSubmission may contain multiple SubmissionAnswers, while each SubmissionAnswer
belongs to one QuizSubmission.
• One Question may be referenced by multiple SubmissionAnswers, while each
SubmissionAnswer refers to one Question.
1.4.2 Entity Relationship Diagram
The Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) illustrates the logical structure of the Quiz Application
database and the relationships among the entities identified during database analysis.
The Quiz, Question, and Answer entities form the core structure used to manage quiz content. The
QuizSubmission and SubmissionAnswer entities extend the data model to support basic quiz
participation and submission.
Primary keys are used to uniquely identify individual records, while foreign keys are used to establish
relationships between related entities and maintain referential integrity.
The ERD uses Crow's Foot notation to represent the cardinality of the relationships among the
entities.
